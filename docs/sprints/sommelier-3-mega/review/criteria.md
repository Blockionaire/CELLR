# What counts as an error — one page for all three reviews

Use these three words the same way in every form.

1. **Factually wrong.** A statement a professional would correct for this wine, vintage or dish: a temperature, a
   decanting advice, a drinking window, a pairing judgement or a label that is not right. "Partly" when part of the
   row is right and part is not; say which part.
2. **Unwarranted certainty.** The words claim more than is known: a rule of thumb stated as a fact about this exact
   bottle, an estimated window given as certain, a clash called a "good" match. Words such as "about", "rule of
   thumb" and "estimate" are meant as hedges: judge whether the hedge is enough.
3. **Missed nuance.** Right in general, but leaves out what matters for this wine or dish (a style the rule does not
   know, an exception every sommelier would mention). Write the nuance in a few words.

When you are not sure, choose **unsure** and write why. An empty cell means "not reviewed", never "agree".
