# Sommelier V1+ — P4 and P5: the `annotate` and `quantities` parts

2026-11-23, a branch from `main` at `b13cf26` (after P2, #123). Plan:
[`../SOMMELIER_CLOSEOUT.md`](../SOMMELIER_CLOSEOUT.md) §6 (P4, P5). No paid model call, no migration, no switch on,
no production change. Both parts are off unless `CELLR_SOMMELIER` names them, and each works without the other.

## 1. What they do

V1's model keeps choosing the wines. Code adds what it can prove from the cellar and the reviewed tables.

| Part         | Adds                                                                                                                                                             | From                                                                                          |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `annotate`   | Per card: drink now or keep (with the window's years and what it rests on), serving temperature and decanting, bottles free to open, places                      | `item.drink` (the window the app already shows), `servingAdvice`, the reservation, the places |
| `annotate`   | For "how do I serve / how long can I keep …": the same facts for the wines named, given to the model, each marked as known or a rule of thumb; their cards shown | `servingFactsBlock`, with the household's own profile of those wines                          |
| `quantities` | For a number of people the person named: bottles needed, and per card how many are free to open and how many short                                               | `guestsIn`, `bottlesFor`, `enoughFor`; reserved bottles are never counted as free             |
| `quantities` | The model is told who is coming and that the app counts, so it states no number of bottles of its own                                                            | `guestsBlock`                                                                                 |

**What does not change.** The wines, their order, the words and the verdict are V1's (`service.test.ts` compares the
answer with the part on and off). For a question that names no wine to serve and no number of people, the prompt is
V1's byte for byte. A question about the cellar itself ("how many", "where") gets neither facts nor a count. The
engines' own plan (`sommelier_engine`, benchmark only) is left as it is.

**What the model is told about the facts.** Each fact in the block is marked. `[as written]` (a researched profile
of the exact wine, the household's own window): state it as written and give no other value. `[rule of thumb]` (the
table for the wine's style): give it as such; the model may add what it knows of the wine as its own view, as long as
it does not contradict the drinking window. A window the app does not know is said to be unknown, with no years.

**The app's facts lead.** After the model has answered, its words are compared in code with the facts on the cards
(`reconcileServingClaims`; no second model call). A sentence that states, as fact, a serving temperature, a decanting
or airing time, or drinking years for a wine on the cards that differ from that card's facts is left out, and the
answer says so (`servingCheck`, codes only). Tolerances: a temperature outside the card's range by more than one
degree; an airing time under half or over twice the card's, or 30 minutes and more where the card says none; a
drinking year more than one year off, or any year where the app says it does not know. When nothing is left of the
words, the answer points at the card. A card without a fact never contradicts.

**Never false certainty.**

- A rule of thumb is worded as one ("about 15–17 °C · rule of thumb"), and years that are an estimate are marked
  ("est. 2022–2031"); only a researched profile of the exact wine, or the household's own window, is stated plainly.
- An old bottle is decanted "gently off the sediment shortly before serving"; whether it also wants air is not
  claimed.
- A wine known by its colour only gets a temperature for that colour and no decanting claim. A fortified wine of
  unknown style gets no serving advice at all (a fino and a vintage port are served ten degrees apart).
- A window the app itself marks as uncertain gives no years and no "drink soon".
- A profile that cannot be read leaves the rules of thumb; a part that fails adds nothing and the answer is kept.
- A guest count is only a number the person stated ("we are 8", "voor 12 personen", "met z'n achten"). "A few
  friends", a year or a number of bottles is not one.

## 2. Reused from #95

`experiences/serving.ts`: no forced air for a firm red of unknown age; a profile's decanting ignored for sparkling,
rosé, sweet wines and bottles of 15 years or more, capped at 240 minutes; `basis: "profile"` only when temperature
and decanting both come from the profile. `sommelier-route.ts`: "how many … need" is a quantity question, a bottle
that is already open is not a keeping question. Not taken: the engines' orchestration, `asksPairing`, `asksWhichOf`.

## 7. Open

- Wording on a real phone was checked in the end-to-end screenshots, not by a person on a device.
- The gate's "checked sample of 50 annotations, reviewed by a person" (closeout §5) has not been done.
- `guestsIn` reads "8 guests" as 8 people; whether the hosts come on top is not known. Corrections are not added up
  ("two more are coming").
- `servingFactsBlock` finds a wine by its producer or name words, or by an appellation or grape; nicknames and
  misspellings are missed, and more than three matching wines give no block rather than a partial one.
- The comparison of the model's words with the card reads numbers only: "well chilled" or "plenty of air" is not
  seen, "it" in a later sentence is not traced back to a wine named earlier, and a temperature without a unit is
  not read. "Room temperature" counts as 20–22 °C.
- "We are 6 with 2 kids" is read as 6: whether the six include the children is a guess either way.
- The serving temperatures and decanting rules are the app's own tables, changed in this round by an AI agent on an
  AI reviewer's remarks. No wine professional has read them (R-01); a sample of 50 annotations for that review is
  prepared with the quality report.
