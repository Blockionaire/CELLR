## Scope and files

`packages/domain/src/food/`:

- `types.ts` — what a dish is: what it is built on (15 classes), weight and intensity (1–5), fat, acid, sweetness,
  salt, bitterness, umami, chili heat, aromatic spice and smoke (0–3), 34 tags for what a structure cannot say
  (raw seafood, tomato, red-wine sauce, blue cheese, artichoke…), provenance, and the profile a reading produces.
- `compose.ts` — the structure of a dish from its parts: the main ingredient sets the base, the technique shifts
  it, what lies beside it adds flavour at reduced strength, the sauce pulls weight and flavour towards itself by
  how much it dominates.
- `parse.ts` — reads a sentence in English or Dutch: longest phrase first (dish, then sauce, technique,
  ingredient), plurals and Dutch diminutives, Dutch compounds ("citroenbotersaus"), verb forms ("we're grilling",
  "we grillen"), negation ("no salmon after all"), the thing named before "with" as what the plate is about, a dish
  named only by its sauce ("a korma") as an assumption, taste words ("very spicy", "mild", "creamy"), and courses
  of a menu.
- `knowledge/` — 331 ingredients, 31 techniques, 82 sauces, 360 dish templates (192 European, 107 from the rest
  of the world, 61 desserts and cheeses), each with English and Dutch names and synonyms.
- Tests: `knowledge.test.ts` (integrity), `parse.test.ts` (reading).

Also: the package entry `@cellr/domain/intelligence` with its three guards (`apps/web/eslint.config.mjs`,
`src/test/architecture.test.ts`, `src/test/domain-entries.test.ts`).

No migration, switch, route or screen. Nothing reaches a user.

## The owner's example

"Pan-seared sea bass with lemon butter sauce" is not a dish template. It is composed: sea bass (white fish,
delicate) + pan-seared + lemon butter sauce → a white-fish dish of weight about 2.5 with clear fat and acidity and
the tags `butter` and `citrus`. The same reading comes out of "gebakken zeebaars met citroenbotersaus".

## Architecture choices

- **A dish is a structure, not a category.** V1 mapped a question to one of 46 dishes by the first keyword that
  matched. The pairing engine needs to know why a wine suits, so the food engine says what is on the plate.
- **Deterministic first.** No model reads the dish. An optional model interpretation (S7) would map onto the same
  vocabulary and never bypass it. Rejected: letting a model describe the dish freely (not reproducible, not
  testable, costs a call per question).
- **Templates name parts; the structure is computed.** A template that says "sea bass, pan-fried, beurre blanc"
  stays right when the composition rules improve. `adjust` exists for where the sum of the parts is off. Rejected:
  hand-written numbers per dish (360 sets of eleven numbers nobody can review).
- **Knowledge is code**, typed and tested, in a server-only entry (D-03).

## Data: where it comes from, and its status

Written for CELLR from general culinary knowledge by an AI engineering agent, under review of the lead agent. No
recipe text, no third-party dataset, no brand names. Every file says so, and the knowledge carries
`FOOD_PROVENANCE = { source: "cellr", reviewed: false }`. **No culinary or wine professional has reviewed it**
(RISK R-01). Quality control so far:

- integrity tests: unique ids, every name normalised, every axis in range, every template's parts exist, no name
  claimed by two entries of the same kind, desserts are sweet and main courses are not;
- reading tests in both languages, including the same dish read the same way in English and Dutch;
- the benchmark's independent dish cards (107, written without sight of this knowledge): main class and five axes
  within one point for 137 of 144 attempted scenarios;
- a shared-vocabulary test: every "classic" a dish names is a key some wine answers to (`vocabulary.test.ts`).
  It found 98 keys in 10 spellings that no wine answered to; they were translated.

## Limitations

- The knowledge is a draft (R-01).
- Words outside the knowledge are ignored, and say so (`unknown`, lower confidence). A dish made only of unknown
  words is not understood; the engine then asks what is being eaten instead of guessing.
- The parser reads up to 600 characters.
- Quantities, cooking times and regional variants of a dish are not modelled.
