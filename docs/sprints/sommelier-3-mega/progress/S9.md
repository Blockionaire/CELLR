### 2.2 A second opinion on the disagreements

The 72 dish and wine pairs on which a label and the S8 engine disagreed were judged blind by an independent
reviewer (an AI reviewer acting as a sommelier; it saw the dish card and the wine card, not the label and not the
engine's level). `results/s9-disputed-pairs.json` holds every pair with its reasons.

| The reviewer sided with | Pairs | Reading                                                                          |
| ----------------------- | ----: | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| the label               |    32 | Real engine errors: 24 good wines under-rated, 8 real clashes called good        |
| the engine              |    14 | The label is debatable (list below)                                              |
| neither                 |    26 | The label says poor, the engine good, the reviewer "ok": the truth is in between |

Applied to the 39 scenarios with a poor bottle offered: in **5** the reviewer agrees it is a clash, in 4 the
scenario mixes both, in **18** the wine is "ok" rather than poor, and in **12** the reviewer calls the labelled-poor
wine good. So the largest class of failures is mostly a disagreement about where "poor" begins, not a table of bad
recommendations.

This reviewer is not a wine professional and its verdicts change no label and no score. They decided one thing:
which engine rules to revisit (the 32), and which to leave alone (the 40).

### 2.3 What changed in the engine

Changed on the reviewer's 32 and on general principles; never on a dish name or a scenario id.

- **Levels need a reason.** "Good" now needs a structural reason that vouches for the match (a classic, a specific
  affinity, tannin meeting meat, freshness with seafood, sweetness with sweetness, salt or heat, bubbles with
  fried food), not only the absence of a fault.
- **Classics by name only.** A classic softens a fault only when the wine is that classic by name or grape, not
  because its region grows it too ("Pinot Noir, Burgundy" no longer makes a white Burgundy a classic).
- **Heat and tannin.** Tannin with chili heat is a fault; the credit for tannin with meat is halved under heat.
- **Weight.** Fresh, low-tannin, unoaked wines tolerate a wider gap in weight; a bone-dry wine with a sweet or hot
  dish is a fault; a fortified wine overpowers a light dessert.
- **Order.** Among equally suitable wines a classic comes first, then the wine closest in body to the dish.
- **Food.** A garnish (bacon, pancetta) no longer takes over a starch dish; a composed plate with no classic of
  its own takes the classics of its parts.
- **Routing.** "Roquefort after dinner, which wine do I have?" asks what to open, not for a list of the cellar.

Two experiments (credit for plain roast meat; acidity as a vouching reason) moved the main set by less than the
noise between runs and were reverted.

### 2.5 What the two tables say together

- The improvement is real but small on unseen dishes: **+5 of 88** (+4 of 43 on the blind half), against +15 of 222
  on the main set.
- **"First bottle" did not move on unseen dishes** (56 → 56 of 88) although it rose from 73% to 83% on the main
  set. That gain is fitted to the main set. The gate "at least 80%" is met there and not in general.
- **Recognition is the first problem**, not pairing: 72% of unseen dishes are understood, against 92% of the main
  set. The food knowledge is narrower than the main set made it look. A dish that is not understood cannot be
  paired, so this caps every other measure.
- On unseen dishes **every quality measure is below its gate**. The main set's "six of eight met" is the optimistic
  reading; the holdout is the honest one.
- Nothing was invented in any run, and the engine's honesty about "nothing suits" holds on both sets.

All three sets are pinned (`benchmark.test.ts`): a change that moves any scenario fails a test until the recorded
result is updated on purpose.

## 3. Product: the brief against what exists

[`PRODUCT_STATUS.md`](../PRODUCT_STATUS.md) is the overview. Built in S9:

**A friend's cellar in the answer** (D-29). Migration `20261127000050_sommelier_friend_cellar.sql`: a second
consent on `sommelier_sharing` (`cellar_for_friends`, off by default) and `sommelier_friend_cellar(handle)`. A
friend's wines are returned only when all three hold in that request: an accepted friendship, the friend shows
their cellar to this person under the existing sharing rules (`private.can_see`), and the friend switched the new
consent on. Returned: producer, wine, vintage, colour, region, appellation, country. Never a count, a place, a
rating or a note. One friend's cellar per question, 120 reads an hour per person. The plan shows their wines as
theirs, with no card and no link to a bottle of mine. Without consent: "I cannot see what @name has in their
cellar, so I say nothing about what they own."

**A present they do not already own.** Styles for the receiver's taste, minus those found among the wines they
share; the answer lists what was left out and says it is "not found in what they share". The giver's own taste is
not used for a present. A bug found on the way: a friend's taste was ignored for a present without a dish; fixed.

**A friend stays in the conversation.** A follow-up that names nobody is about the friend the conversation named.
The name is read from the asker's own earlier questions, never from the state and never from an answer; who that
is and what they allow is asked of the database again in every request, so consent withdrawn between two turns
ends it at the next question. A follow-up that turns to the asker's own wines leaves the friend's cellar alone.
Nothing that names or uses a friend is kept or sent to a model, follow-ups included.

Marked **BLOCKED**, not done: live availability online and in shops (no licensed source), the review by a wine
professional (a person), the measurement of V1 with AI (a key and approval).
