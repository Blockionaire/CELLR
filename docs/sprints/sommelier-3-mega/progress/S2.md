## Scope and files

`packages/domain/src/pairing-engine/`:

- `wine.ts` — what a wine is like at table (body, tannin, acidity, sweetness, intensity, oak, alcohol, bubbles)
  from the best evidence there is, and the tier of that evidence: `exact` (a researched profile that qualifies as
  fact about the exact wine and states at least three of the four scales), `appellation` (its recorded appellation
  has a rule), `estimate` (only its region, country or grape is known), `unknown` (no origin recorded; its name
  may shape the guess, the tier does not rise for it).
- `knowledge/` — 566 rules: 379 for appellations and label words (Kabinett, Fino, Gran Reserva), 101 for regions,
  86 for grapes. AI-written, `reviewed: false`.
- `assess.ts` — one wine with one dish. Every rule leaves a note; the notes are the explanation. Levels: good,
  acceptable (it will do, and here is why), neutral (nothing against it, nothing for it), poor. Five hard clashes
  make a pairing poor whatever else is true: tannin with raw or delicate fish, tannin with chili heat, tannin
  with a sweet dish, a dry wine with a dessert, a sweet wine with an ordinary savoury dish.
- `decide.ts` — the outcome: good, acceptable or none. Only wines of the best level are offered (at most three);
  a neutral wine is never offered and at most named as the closest there is. Taste orders wines of one level.
  Confidence is the least of how well the dish was read and how well the least-known offered wine is known.
- `taste.ts` — a person's taste as closed signals and a score from -1 to 1 for ordering.
- Tests: `assess.test.ts` (rules and decision, hand-stated dishes and wines), `knowledge.test.ts` (integrity,
  readings, tiers, name collisions), `taste.test.ts`, `../classics.test.ts` (46 textbook pairings and 15 textbook
  clashes on the real knowledge), `../vocabulary.test.ts`.
- Benchmark: `scripts/sommelier-eval/engines/v3.ts` (the engines alone), `matrix.ts` (every dish card against
  every wine, a development half and a holdout half).

## Architecture choices

- **Rules with notes, not a learned score.** Explainable by construction and testable rule by rule. Rejected: a
  weighted distance between two vectors (V1's method: it cannot say why, and cannot say no).
- **Suitability decides the level; taste only orders inside a level.** Enforced in the sort, proven by a property
  test over every dish and taste, and attacked by the reviewer with 4,000 random decisions (no violation found).
- **Honest "none".** A wine is offered only when something speaks for it. V1 always returned three bottles.
- **A number that is never shown.** The score puts wines in order; no screen and no API field presents it as a
  percentage or a degree of compatibility.

### The missed thresholds, investigated

The thresholds were set in S0, before the engine existed, and are not changed. What the misses are:

1. **Ties and near-ties.** Of the 17 development dishes whose first pick is not labelled good, 7 have a
   labelled-good wine within two points of the first (roast chicken: Beaujolais and red Burgundy at the same score;
   sea bream: Muscadet level with three labelled-good whites). The check counts these as misses.
2. **Labels that call a sound wine poor.** The reference lists three to five good and three to five acceptable
   wines per dish; the other twenty count as poor. Cava with scallops, Provence rosé with roast chicken and
   Sancerre with charcuterie are "poor" by that rule. The engine offers some of them, with reasons a sommelier would
   accept. This is label noise the benchmark cannot separate from engine error without a professional's review
   (R-01, R-11).
3. **Real engine weaknesses**, visible in the development half: firm, high-acid Italian reds score too well with
   moderately rich dishes (Barolo with a burger); light fresh wines the reference prefers with heavy Dutch winter
   dishes (stamppot, pea soup) are marked too light; tomato-based pasta ranks crisp whites over the light reds the
   reference prefers.

A tuned rule set fits the half it was tuned on better than the other (79.6% against 76.5%): the gap is the
measured overfitting, and it is small. The holdout is independent of the rule tuning, not of the knowledge: see
finding 8.

## Limitations

- Knowledge and labels are AI-written and not reviewed by a professional (R-01).
- Two S0 thresholds are missed (first bottle, no poor bottle): R-11.
- The acceptable level is a narrow band (68 to 71): 13% of all pairs score within two points of a threshold, so
  small changes to the knowledge move wines across levels. The textbook test guards the pairings that matter most.
- Vintage variation, bottle age (beyond the drinking window the app already has), sweetness levels within one
  appellation (Vouvray), and producer style are not modelled.
- Not wired into the app: no person can reach any of this yet.
