# Sommelier V1+ — P3: the `check` part

2026-11-23, a branch from `main` at `b13cf26` (after P2, #123). Plan:
[`../SOMMELIER_CLOSEOUT.md`](../SOMMELIER_CLOSEOUT.md) §6 (P3). No paid model call, no migration, no switch on, no
production change. The part is off unless `CELLR_SOMMELIER` names `check`; without it V1 asks with prompt v6 and
shows its answer exactly as before.

## 1. What it does

V1's model keeps choosing the wines. The part decides which of its **words** can be shown. It is deliberately not a
filter on wine names: the sommelier may compare wines and talk about wines the person does not own, as long as it
does not present them as owned.

| Layer                           | What                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Structure (prompt v7)        | A cellar wine is written as its ref (`[[a1b2c3]]`); code renders the producer, name and vintage from the cellar. The schema has no price field in `buy`              |
| 2. Claims, sentence by sentence | English and Dutch: a wine presented as owned that is not in the cellar; a vintage or a number of bottles the cellar does not have; money; a shop or stock; an action |
| 3. Buying                       | The model's price is never shown: the person's own budget band, or "price not known". Clauses with money or a shop are dropped; a wine already owned is not a buy    |

| Found                                                                         | Shown                                                                                        |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Nothing                                                                       | The model's words, with refs rendered                                                        |
| `price`, `availability`, `action`                                             | That sentence is left out, with a short note; if nothing is left, as below                   |
| `not-in-cellar` by name only (no vintage, no number of bottles)               | That sentence is left out, with a short note; if nothing is left, as below                   |
| `unknown-ref`, `wrong-vintage`, `wrong-count`, `not-in-cellar` with a vintage | The words are replaced by one sentence made from the first card and the verdict, with a note |
| The same, for a question about the cellar itself                              | The app's own counted answer (`plainFacts`), never a sentence about a card                   |

An answer is never without words: when the model's are replaced, `text` holds the card sentence in the person's
language (`parts/check-words.ts`, from the same messages the screen uses), so a screen from before this part, or the
app after a rollback, still draws and sends back something true.

The cards (V1's choice) and the verdict are never changed by the check. The answer carries `check: { v, text, codes }`
(codes only, never the removed words), and that is what is kept with the conversation.

**Prompt v7 is v6 with three enumerated edits** (`V7_EDITS`, tested: undoing them gives v6 back byte for byte): refs
in the answer and no number of bottles or year beyond the wine's line; buying without a price, a shop or
availability; reserving and ordering added to what the sommelier cannot do. The user message is identical to v6's.

## 3. Tests

- Domain: 41 rule tests (each rule flagged and surviving, English and Dutch) and 33 adversarial ones: near-miss
  names, a real wine with a wrong vintage written six ways, wrong counts in digits and words, fifteen price
  notations, text that looks like an injected instruction, very long text, a 3,000-wine cellar, an empty cellar,
  blank data, wines sharing a producer, NV wines, names with years or numbers in them, refs glued to punctuation.
- `service.test.ts`: 13 new cases. v7 only with the part; refs rendered; each cellar-fact failure replaces the words
  and leaves the cards and the verdict; comparisons and non-owned wines kept; a price sentence trimmed; buying with
  and without a budget; the part off shows V1's answer price and all; a question of fact; Dutch at the end of a
  twelve-message conversation; an empty cellar.
- `e2e/sommelier-check.spec.ts` (phone and desktop, features server): refs rendered; an invented bottle and a wrong
  vintage replaced, kept after a reload, and never sent along with the next question; Tonight in Dutch at 360 px with
  the budget line and no price; accessibility; no sideways scroll; and both servers over the API (v6 with V1's
  answer as it was against v7 with the check).
- `e2e/sommelier-parts.spec.ts` is replaced by the comparison above: with `check` on, the features server's prompt
  is v7 by design.

## 4. The independent end review (2026-11-23) and what was fixed

With the part off nothing changed against `main` (912 compared runs, 0 differences). With it on, the review found
everyday sentences that lost a whole good answer, and invented claims that were kept. Fixed after it:

| Finding                                                                                                            | Now                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "Your Riedel glasses", "your Indonesian rijsttafel", "your Tuscany trip" read as a wine that is not owned          | A name counts as a wine only when something says it is one (a wine word, a vintage, an appellation or grape, a cellar producer) |
| One doubtful name replaced the whole answer                                                                        | A name-only `not-in-cellar` removes its own sentence; with a vintage or a count it still replaces the words                     |
| Dutch "open je twee flessen …" read as a stock claim                                                               | A suggestion, flagged only when it asks for more than the cellar holds                                                          |
| "Serve / pour / grab the Sassicaia", "from the rack" kept for a wine that is not there                             | The imperative and place rules cover them, in both languages                                                                    |
| "I've set the Barolo aside", "ik heb … in de koelkast gelegd", "is now reserved" kept                              | Caught as actions                                                                                                               |
| "Your Pinot Noir" owned in a cellar without one                                                                    | A grape is owned only when a cellar wine's own words name it                                                                    |
| Vintages and counts in other forms ("the 2008,", "from the 2008 vintage", "(×9)", "9 bottles … remain")            | Read                                                                                                                            |
| Prices without a currency sign ("25 a bottle"), other currencies, more shops                                       | Read                                                                                                                            |
| A ref in extra brackets replaced the answer; a ref cut off by the length limit was shown raw                       | The first renders; the second is dropped with its unfinished sentence                                                           |
| 279 ms per check at 3,000 wines (the index was rebuilt, and compared every group with every group)                 | 43 ms for the first check, 0.02 ms for each further one on the same question (`prepareAnswerCheck`)                             |
| An answer whose words were replaced had no text: an older screen drew an empty bubble and its next question failed | `text` always holds the card sentence                                                                                           |

Measured after the fixes, on sets kept as tests (`sommelier-check.corpus.test.ts`): 0 of 60 everyday answers and 0 of
40 weak-spot sentences flagged; 40 of 40 invented claims caught. The sets were written by the agents that built and
reviewed the part, not drawn from real answers of the model.

## 5. What is not proven

- **The effect of prompt v7 on V1's choice has not been measured.** It changes what the model is asked; the closeout
  gates this part on a paid comparison of v7 against v6 on unseen dishes (§5), and no paid call has been approved
  (D-51). Until that run, the part must not be switched on for real people.
- Whether the model follows the ref rule is likewise unmeasured. If it writes names instead, layer 2 still checks
  them, with the limits below.
- The claim rules are heuristics. They prefer missing a doubtful claim over removing a good answer; the module's
  header lists what each cannot see. The main ones: a vintage in another sentence than the wine's name, cellar-wide
  totals, a near-miss name stated without an ownership phrase, spelled-out amounts without a currency word, a shop
  described in other words.
- Known misses kept on purpose (pinned in the corpus test): a bare name with nothing that marks it as a wine ("Your
  Krug is ready."), a vintage in another sentence than the name, a shop named without a buying verb.
- Remaining false-positive risk: a capitalised dish or brand that is in none of the lists, at the end of a sentence
  after "pour" or "open the".
- Nothing compares a temperature, a decanting time or a drinking year in the model's words with the facts the
  `annotate` part puts on the card.
